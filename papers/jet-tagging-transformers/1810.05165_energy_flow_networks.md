# Energy Flow Networks: Deep Sets for Particle Jets

- **Authors:** Patrick T. Komiske, Eric M. Metodiev, Jesse Thaler
- **Venue / Year:** JHEP 01 (2019) 121; 2019 (arXiv 2018)
- **arXiv:** 1810.05165 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05165
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.05165
- **Code:** EnergyFlow package — https://energyflow.network (https://github.com/pkomiske/EnergyFlow)
- **Topic area:** Jet tagging & transformers

## TL;DR
Adapts the **Deep Sets** framework to jets, defining **Energy Flow Networks (EFN)** — permutation-invariant, infrared-and-collinear (IRC) safe by construction — and the more general **Particle Flow Networks (PFN)** that allow arbitrary energy dependence and extra per-particle features (charge, flavor). Provides a theoretically grounded, permutation-symmetric per-particle latent representation summed over constituents.

## Summary
The paper brings the machine-learning idea of learning directly from sets of features to particle physics. It builds on the Deep Sets theorem to construct architectures with a per-particle internal (latent) representation that is summed over all particles to form an event/jet-level representation, then mapped to an output. Energy Flow Networks enforce infrared and collinear safety by construction (energy enters only as a linear weight on a function of the angular coordinates), making them robust observables. Particle Flow Networks relax IRC safety to permit general energy dependence and additional particle-level information such as charge and flavor. The latent space is shown to be interpretable and connected to existing physics observables.

## Key methods / contributions
- **Deep Sets for jets**: per-particle map Φ summed over constituents, then global map F — a permutation-invariant, variable-multiplicity architecture.
- **Energy Flow Network (EFN)**: energy-weighted sum of a learned function of angular coordinates → **IRC-safe by construction**.
- **Particle Flow Network (PFN)**: general per-particle inputs (incl. charge/flavor), dropping the IRC-safety restriction for higher performance.
- Demonstrates an interpretable latent space and links to known jet observables.
- Released the widely used `EnergyFlow` Python package.

## Main results
EFNs and PFNs are competitive with other modern jet-tagging architectures on standard discrimination tasks (e.g. quark/gluon), with PFNs generally outperforming IRC-safe EFNs due to access to additional per-particle information (confirmed qualitatively). Specific AUC values: see paper.

## Relevance to BNJetTag
EFN/PFN are the foundational **permutation-invariant set / Deep Sets** approach underpinning all particle-cloud taggers, including the transformer family BNJetTag binarizes — the "sum over per-particle embeddings" is conceptually the pooling step in our model. The Deep Sets symmetry argument motivates why our BitNet transformer must remain permutation-invariant over constituents. PFNs are also a lightweight architecture relevant to the L1-trigger resource budget; understanding their accuracy floor helps frame how much our 1-bit model can give up. IRC safety is a useful robustness lens for any feature we feed the quantized model.
