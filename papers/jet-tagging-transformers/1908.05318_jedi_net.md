# JEDI-net: a jet identification algorithm based on interaction networks

- **Authors:** Eric A. Moreno, Olmo Cerri, Javier M. Duarte, Harvey B. Newman, Thong Q. Nguyen, Avikar Periwal, Maurizio Pierini, Aidana Serikova, Maria Spiropulu, Jean-Roch Vlimant
- **Venue / Year:** Eur. Phys. J. C 80, 58; 2020 (arXiv 2019)
- **arXiv:** 1908.05318 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05318
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.05318
- **Code:** https://github.com/jmduarte/JEDInet-code
- **Topic area:** Jet tagging & transformers

## TL;DR
Applies **interaction networks (INs)** — a graph/relational architecture modeling pairwise constituent interactions — to all-hadronic boosted jet tagging (q/g, W, Z, top). JEDI-net describes jet dynamics as one-to-one interactions between constituents and outperforms dense, convolutional, and recurrent baselines, while keeping a relatively compact, permutation-invariant design.

## Summary
JEDI-net identifies all-hadronic decays of high-momentum heavy particles (W/Z/Higgs bosons, top quarks) produced at the LHC and distinguishes them from ordinary quark/gluon jets. The jet is represented as a set of constituents whose dynamics are modeled as one-to-one (pairwise) interactions via an interaction network: a learned relational function over particle pairs produces interaction effects that are aggregated per particle, then pooled to a permutation-invariant jet representation for classification. On a benchmark dataset of jets from light quarks, gluons, vector bosons, and top quarks, JEDI-net achieves better performance than models based on dense, convolutional, and recurrent neural networks operating on the same inputs.

## Key methods / contributions
- **Interaction Network** for jets: explicit **pairwise constituent interaction** modeling (relational inductive bias), aggregated and pooled permutation-invariantly.
- Two variants studied, including one using a summed (order-invariant) aggregation of post-interaction particle features.
- Benchmarked against DNN, CNN, and RNN baselines on the same multi-class jet dataset (q/g, W, Z, top).
- Compact constituent-level architecture relevant to **trigger / low-latency** deployment (this group is closely tied to hls4ml/FPGA L1 work).

## Main results
JEDI-net achieves higher tagging performance (accuracy / AUC across the q/g, W, Z, top classes) than dense, CNN, and RNN baselines on the same inputs (confirmed qualitatively). Specific per-class AUC and accuracy values, and parameter counts: see paper.

## Relevance to BNJetTag
JEDI-net is a **direct precursor to BNJetTag's deployment thesis**: it comes from the same hls4ml/CMS-trigger community (Duarte, Pierini et al.), targets multi-class constituent-level jet tagging, and emphasizes compact models suitable for the L1 latency budget. Its pairwise-interaction mechanism is conceptually the relational bias that attention provides in our transformer — useful context for what we keep or drop under binarization. The q/g/W/Z/top benchmark and its reported AUCs are a relevant comparison set, and JEDI-net's resource/latency framing prefigures our "~0 DSP via {−1,+1} weights" goal.
