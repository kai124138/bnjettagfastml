# An Efficient Lorentz Equivariant Graph Neural Network for Jet Tagging

- **Authors:** Shiqi Gong, Qi Meng, Jue Zhang, Huilin Qu, Congqiao Li, Sitian Qian, Weitao Du, Zhi-Ming Ma, Tie-Yan Liu
- **Venue / Year:** JHEP 07 (2022) 030; 2022
- **arXiv:** 2201.08187 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08187
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.08187
- **Code:** https://github.com/sdogsq/LorentzNet-release
- **Topic area:** Jet tagging & transformers

## TL;DR
Introduces **LorentzNet**, a Lorentz-group-equivariant graph neural network for jet tagging whose message passing uses an efficient **Minkowski dot-product attention** over particle pairs, avoiding the costly high-order tensor constructions of earlier equivariant models. It reaches state-of-the-art tagging accuracy and, thanks to the Lorentz-symmetry inductive bias, stays competitive even when trained on very few jets.

## Summary
Lorentz-group equivariance is a fundamental spacetime symmetry of elementary particles and a powerful inductive bias for jet tagging, but prior equivariant designs were computationally expensive because they analytically built high-order tensors. LorentzNet keeps full Lorentz equivariance while being efficient: it updates geometric (Minkowski) tensors via a Minkowski dot-product attention that aggregates pairwise features weighted by an embedding of the Minkowski inner product. On two representative jet-tagging benchmarks (top tagging and quark/gluon discrimination), LorentzNet achieves the best performance and improves significantly over prior state-of-the-art, while the symmetry bias greatly improves data efficiency and generalization — competitive results are obtained with only a few thousand training jets (and even ~0.5% of the training set).

## Key methods / contributions
- **Lorentz-equivariant GNN** that respects the SO(1,3) spacetime symmetry of particle four-momenta.
- **Minkowski dot-product attention**: efficient message passing using pairwise Minkowski inner products as attention weights, avoiding explicit high-order tensors.
- Strong **data efficiency / generalization** from the symmetry inductive bias (competitive with only thousands of jets).
- State-of-the-art results on the top-tagging reference dataset and quark/gluon benchmark.
- Public implementation released.

## Main results
LorentzNet achieves the best tagging performance on both the top-tagging and quark/gluon benchmarks, improving over previous SOTA (e.g. ParticleNet) on accuracy, AUC, and background rejection, and retains competitive performance under drastically reduced training data (confirmed qualitatively). Specific numeric AUC / 1/εB values: see paper.

## Relevance to BNJetTag
LorentzNet is a top-of-leaderboard **full-precision baseline** on the same top-tagging and quark/gluon benchmarks BNJetTag targets, so its AUC/background-rejection numbers help define the accuracy ceiling for our 1-bit model. Its **physics-symmetry inductive bias** is a strategically important contrast to our approach: where LorentzNet spends representational power on exact Lorentz equivariance, BNJetTag spends its budget on extreme weight quantization for FPGA efficiency — a useful framing for the accuracy-vs-resource trade-off. Its data-efficiency result is also relevant if our binarized model needs symmetry-aware features to compensate for reduced capacity. Shares several authors with Particle Transformer/ParticleNet (the architecture family we binarize).
