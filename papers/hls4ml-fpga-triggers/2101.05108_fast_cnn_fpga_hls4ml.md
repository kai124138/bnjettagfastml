# Fast convolutional neural networks on FPGAs with hls4ml

- **Authors:** T. Aarrestad, V. Loncar, N. Ghielmetti, M. Pierini, S. Summers, J. Ngadiuba, C. Petersson, H. Linander, Y. Iiyama, G. Di Guglielmo, J. Duarte, P. Harris, D. Rankin, S. Jindariani, K. Pedro, N. Tran, M. Liu, E. Kreinar, Z. Wu, D. Hoang
- **Venue / Year:** Machine Learning: Science and Technology 2 (2021) 045015 — published 2021 (arXiv Jan 2021)
- **arXiv:** 2101.05108 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.05108
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.05108
- **Code:** https://github.com/fastmachinelearning/hls4ml
- **Topic area:** hls4ml & FPGA triggers

## TL;DR
Extends hls4ml to support convolutional neural networks for ultra-low-latency, low-power FPGA inference. Using a benchmark CNN trained on the Street View House Numbers dataset, it demonstrates ~5 µs inference and shows that pruning plus quantization-aware training shrinks resource usage with little to no accuracy loss.

## Summary
This paper introduces an automated tool, built by extending the hls4ml library, for deploying ultra-low-latency, low-power deep neural networks with convolutional layers onto FPGAs. Targeting microsecond-latency applications such as the LHC trigger/DAQ, the authors benchmark convolutional architectures on the SVHN dataset and demonstrate an inference latency on the order of 5 µs. They systematically apply model-compression techniques — pruning and quantization-aware training (via QKeras) — to fit the tight compute and memory constraints of a typical trigger FPGA, showing that resource utilization (DSP/LUT/FF/BRAM) can be significantly reduced while keeping accuracy essentially intact.

## Key methods / contributions
- Adds streaming/serialized **convolutional layer** support (Conv1D/Conv2D) to hls4ml for FPGA deployment.
- Benchmarks on the SVHN image dataset as a realistic CNN test case for trigger-like constraints.
- Demonstrates ~5 µs inference latency for the convolutional architecture (see paper).
- Combines pruning + quantization-aware training (QKeras) to cut resources with minimal accuracy loss.
- Reports detailed FPGA resource/latency scans across precision and compression settings.

## Main results
The convolutional benchmark achieves microsecond-scale (~5 µs) latency on FPGA, and the combination of pruning and low-bit QAT yields large reductions in resource utilization at near-baseline accuracy (exact resource/accuracy tables in the paper).

## Relevance to BNJetTag
While BNJetTag uses a transformer rather than a CNN, this paper is a key reference for how hls4ml handles more complex, layered architectures (beyond simple MLPs) within the FPGA latency budget — directly relevant to mapping our attention/feed-forward blocks. Its joint pruning + QKeras-QAT recipe and its careful DSP/LUT/BRAM accounting are templates for our own resource analysis, and its demonstration that aggressive compression preserves accuracy reinforces the BitNet thesis of trading numeric precision for FPGA-logic efficiency.
