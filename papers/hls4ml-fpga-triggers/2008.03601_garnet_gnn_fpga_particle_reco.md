# Distance-Weighted Graph Neural Networks on FPGAs for Real-Time Particle Reconstruction in High Energy Physics

- **Authors:** Y. Iiyama, G. Cerminara, A. Gupta, J. Kieseler, V. Loncar, M. Pierini, S. R. Qasim, M. Rieger, S. Summers, G. Van Onsem, K. Wozniak, J. Ngadiuba, G. Di Guglielmo, J. Duarte, P. Harris, D. Rankin, S. Jindariani, M. Liu, K. Pedro, N. Tran, E. Kreinar, Z. Wu (Iiyama et al.)
- **Venue / Year:** Frontiers in Big Data 3 (2020) 598927 — published 2020 (arXiv Aug 2020)
- **arXiv:** 2008.03601 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03601
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.03601
- **Code:** https://github.com/fastmachinelearning/hls4ml (GarNet layer integration)
- **Topic area:** hls4ml & FPGA triggers

## TL;DR
Implements distance-weighted graph neural networks (the GarNet architecture) as FPGA firmware via hls4ml, achieving sub-microsecond latency. It shows that GNN-based particle reconstruction — well suited to irregular detector geometries — can meet the strict latency and resource constraints of real-time LHC data filtering.

## Summary
Graph neural networks perform very well on core particle-physics tasks (tracking, jet tagging, clustering) because they naturally handle irregular, non-grid detector data. This paper studies whether such networks can run in the FPGA-based real-time data-filtering (trigger) systems at the LHC, where latency must be below ~1 µs and resources are scarce. The authors take GarNet — a distance-weighted GNN aggregation layer — as their architecture, and build an FPGA firmware implementation of the GarNet layer integrated into the hls4ml library using Vivado HLS. They evaluate it on a representative particle-reconstruction problem and characterize the latency–resource trade-offs needed to fit within the trigger budget.

## Key methods / contributions
- First FPGA firmware implementation of a **distance-weighted GNN (GarNet)** layer, integrated into hls4ml via Vivado HLS.
- Targets and achieves **< 1 µs** latency suitable for the LHC real-time trigger.
- Demonstrates GNNs on irregular-geometry detector data (point-cloud-like inputs) rather than fixed grids.
- Quantifies FPGA resource utilization (DSP/LUT/FF) vs. latency and problem size for the GNN.
- Provides a reusable hls4ml building block for graph-based real-time reconstruction.

## Main results
The GarNet-based network is shown to execute with latency below 1 µs on FPGA while preserving reconstruction performance adequate for the benchmark task; detailed resource and latency figures across configurations are reported in the paper.

## Relevance to BNJetTag
BNJetTag's transformer treats jet constituents as a set/graph of tokens, conceptually close to GarNet's distance-weighted aggregation over an unordered point cloud — so this paper is a strong precedent for getting set/graph-style, permutation-aware architectures through hls4ml under the L1-trigger ~1 µs budget. Its accounting of how aggregation operations consume FPGA DSP/LUT resources informs where our binary-weight approach can offload multiplications to logic, and it validates that non-MLP architectures can be synthesized within trigger latency.
