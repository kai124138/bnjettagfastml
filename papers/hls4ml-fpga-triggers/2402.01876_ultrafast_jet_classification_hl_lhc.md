- **Authors:** P. Odagiu et al. (16 authors)

- **arXiv:** 2402.01876 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01876
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.01876
- **Code:** none found (uses hls4ml; check author repos)
- **Topic area:** hls4ml & FPGA triggers

## TL;DR
Benchmarks three jet-classification architectures — an MLP, a Deep Sets network, and an Interaction Network — synthesized to FPGA via hls4ml for the HL-LHC trigger. With quantization-aware training it achieves O(100) ns inference of these complex (set/graph) models at modest resource cost, and finds Deep Sets the best balance of accuracy, latency, and resources.

## Summary
The paper studies jet-origin classification optimized for FPGA deployment in the hardware trigger layers of an LHC experiment after the high-luminosity upgrade. Three ML models — a baseline MLP, a permutation-invariant Deep Sets network, and an Interaction Network (fully-connected graph) — are trained with quantization-aware training and compiled to firmware using hls4ml, then synthesized with AMD/Xilinx Vivado HLS 2020.1 targeting a Virtex UltraScale+ VU13P FPGA at 200 MHz (representative HL-LHC trigger hardware). The authors show that O(100) ns inference of the complex set/graph architectures is feasible at relatively low resource cost, and analyze how input representation (set vs. fully-connected graph vs. flat MLP) affects accuracy as a function of the number of jet constituents.

## Key methods / contributions
- Direct apples-to-apples FPGA comparison of **MLP vs. Deep Sets vs. Interaction Network** for jet tagging.
- Demonstrates **O(100) ns** inference for set/graph architectures via QAT + hls4ml on a VU13P at 200 MHz.
- Shows set/graph representations beat the flat MLP once the number of jet constituents exceeds ~8.
- Identifies **Deep Sets** as the best accuracy/latency/resource trade-off among the three.
- Provides full firmware resource (DSP/LUT/FF/BRAM) and latency tables for HL-LHC-representative hardware.

## Main results
O(100) ns inference is achieved for Deep Sets and Interaction Networks at relatively low resource usage on a VU13P at 200 MHz; accuracy improves with set/graph representations for jet-constituent multiplicity above ~8, and Deep Sets gives the best overall balance (full numbers in the paper).

## Relevance to BNJetTag
This is the closest direct comparison to BNJetTag's goal: ultra-low-latency, permutation-aware jet tagging on HL-LHC trigger FPGAs via hls4ml. Its Deep Sets and Interaction Network baselines are the natural points of comparison for our binary transformer, and its VU13P-at-200 MHz resource/latency tables set the competitive bar for our LUT/FF/DSP and ~1 µs latency claims. The finding that set/graph representations help for larger constituent counts motivates our attention-based token architecture, and our BitNet thesis aims to undercut these models' DSP usage by replacing multipliers with binary logic at comparable tagging efficiency.
