# Fast inference of deep neural networks in FPGAs for particle physics

- **Authors:** J. Duarte, S. Han, P. Harris, S. Jindariani, E. Kreinar, B. Kreis, J. Ngadiuba, M. Pierini, R. Rivera, N. Tran, Z. Wu
- **Venue / Year:** JINST 13 (2018) P07027 — published 2018 (submitted Apr 2018)
- **arXiv:** 1804.06913 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06913
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.06913
- **Code:** https://github.com/fastmachinelearning/hls4ml
- **Topic area:** hls4ml & FPGA triggers

## TL;DR
The foundational hls4ml paper: it introduces a High-Level-Synthesis-based toolflow that compiles trained neural networks directly into FPGA firmware for sub-microsecond inference. Using a jet-substructure classifier as the case study, it shows deep networks can run within the latency and resource budgets of the LHC Level-1 trigger.

## Summary
The paper addresses how to bring powerful machine-learning methods into the extremely low-latency (sub-microsecond), FPGA-based trigger and data-acquisition systems of LHC experiments. The authors present `hls4ml`, a software package that translates models from common training frameworks into HLS code targeting FPGAs. As a concrete benchmark they implement a fully-connected neural network classifier for jet substructure — relevant to searches for new dark-sector particles and Higgs measurements — and study the trade-offs between latency, resource utilization (DSP/LUT/FF), and accuracy, introducing tunable knobs such as network compression/pruning, quantization (fixed-point precision), and a "reuse factor" that controls parallelism vs. resource reuse.

## Key methods / contributions
- Introduces the `hls4ml` library: an automated NN → HLS → FPGA firmware compiler for particle-physics triggers.
- Demonstrates a jet-substructure (5-class) tagger as a benchmark, achieving inference latency on the order of ~100 ns (O(75–100) ns; see paper).
- Defines the **reuse factor** knob to trade off parallelism (latency/throughput) against FPGA resource usage.
- Studies fixed-point quantization of weights/activations and its effect on accuracy and DSP usage.
- Applies network pruning/compression to fit tight FPGA resource budgets with minimal accuracy loss.

## Main results
The benchmark jet-substructure classifier is shown to fit on a typical trigger FPGA with latency at the ~100 ns scale (see paper for exact tables). The study quantifies how reuse factor, quantization bit-width, and pruning move the design along the latency–resource–accuracy frontier; specific DSP/LUT/latency numbers are reported in the paper's resource tables.

## Relevance to BNJetTag
This is the origin of the entire firmware flow BNJetTag depends on. Our 1-bit BitNet transformer is compiled through this same hls4ml → Vitis HLS pipeline, and the paper's framing of the latency–resource–accuracy trade-off (reuse factor, quantization, pruning) is exactly the design space we operate in. Its DSP-usage analysis of quantized dense layers is the direct precursor to our thesis that binary {−1,+1} weights bypass DSP multipliers and map onto LUT/logic, achieving near-zero DSP while preserving tagging efficiency under the L1-trigger ~1 µs budget.
