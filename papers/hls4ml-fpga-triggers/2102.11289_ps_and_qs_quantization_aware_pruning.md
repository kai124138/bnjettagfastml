# Ps and Qs: Quantization-aware pruning for efficient low latency neural network inference

- **Authors:** B. Hawks, J. Duarte, N. J. Fraser, A. Pappalardo, N. Tran, Y. Umuroglu
- **Venue / Year:** Frontiers in AI 4 (2021) 676564 — published 2021 (arXiv Feb 2021); FERMILAB-PUB-21-056-SCD
- **arXiv:** 2102.11289 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11289
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.11289
- **Code:** none found (built on QKeras / hls4ml / Brevitas tooling)
- **Topic area:** hls4ml & FPGA triggers

## TL;DR
Studies how pruning and quantization interact when both are pushed to the extremes needed for low-latency FPGA inference. It compares pruning before/during/after quantization-aware training and shows that quantization-aware pruning yields the most efficient accuracy-vs-resource trade-off for ultra-compressed networks.

## Summary
Both pruning (removing weights) and quantization (reducing bit-width) compress neural networks, but they are usually treated separately. This paper investigates their interplay in the regime of efficient, low-latency inference, asking when and how to prune relative to quantization-aware training. The authors systematically benchmark strategies — including pruning applied jointly with QAT ("quantization-aware pruning") versus sequential approaches — and evaluate accuracy, sparsity, and hardware cost. They connect the results to the hls4ml/QKeras ecosystem so the findings translate into real FPGA resource and latency savings for particle-physics triggers and similar edge applications.

## Key methods / contributions
- Defines and evaluates **quantization-aware pruning (QAP)** — co-optimizing sparsity and low bit-width during training.
- Compares pruning before vs. during vs. after quantization-aware training.
- Connects to gradient-based and regularization-based pruning and very-low-precision quantization.
- Maps compression gains to FPGA hardware cost via the hls4ml flow.
- Provides guidance on the most resource-efficient pruning+quantization recipe for low-latency inference.

## Main results
Quantization-aware pruning is found to give the best accuracy-vs-efficiency frontier among the strategies tested, enabling high sparsity at low bit-width with minimal accuracy loss and corresponding reductions in FPGA resource usage (quantitative sparsity/accuracy/resource numbers in the paper).

## Relevance to BNJetTag
BNJetTag's headline lever is extreme quantization (1-bit weights), and this paper is the reference for combining that with pruning to squeeze further resource savings without losing tagging accuracy. Its finding that pruning is best done *aware of* quantization is directly actionable for our QKeras training: if we sparsify the binary transformer, we should do it jointly with the binarization, not afterward. The hardware-cost mapping reinforces our LUT/FF/DSP budgeting for the L1 trigger.
