# Automatic heterogeneous quantization of deep neural networks for low-latency inference on the edge for particle detectors

- **Authors:** C. N. Coelho Jr., A. Kuusela, S. Li, H. Zhuang, T. Aarrestad, V. Loncar, J. Ngadiuba, M. Pierini, A. A. Pol, S. Summers
- **Venue / Year:** Nature Machine Intelligence 3 (2021) 675–686 — published Jun 2021 (arXiv Jun 2020)
- **arXiv:** 2006.10159 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10159
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.10159
- **Code:** https://github.com/google/qkeras
- **Topic area:** hls4ml & FPGA triggers

## TL;DR
Introduces QKeras, AutoQKeras, and QTools — libraries for quantization-aware training and automatic, per-layer/per-parameter heterogeneous bit-width selection. The method co-optimizes accuracy against model energy and size to deliver nanosecond-scale, minimum-energy inference deployable on FPGAs for particle detectors.

## Summary
Edge inference for particle detectors demands very small, low-latency, low-energy models, and quantization (fewer bits per weight/activation) is a primary lever. The paper presents a method to design optimally *heterogeneously* quantized networks — each layer and parameter type can take a different bit-width — via an automated procedure that samples from a wide range of quantizers and minimizes energy and size while maintaining accuracy. The tooling (QKeras for QAT, AutoQKeras for automated bit-width search, QTools for energy/size estimation) integrates with the hls4ml flow so that the resulting quantized models deploy directly to chip with nanosecond latency, targeting the strictly resource- and latency-limited event-selection (trigger) stage of LHC proton–proton collisions.

## Key methods / contributions
- **QKeras**: a drop-in QKeras/Keras extension for quantization-aware training with a rich quantizer zoo (including very low bit-widths and binary/ternary).
- **AutoQKeras**: automated per-layer, per-parameter-type heterogeneous quantization search that jointly optimizes accuracy vs. energy/size.
- **QTools**: estimates model energy consumption and size to drive the optimization objective.
- Tight integration with hls4ml for fully automated deployment to FPGA at nanosecond latency.
- Demonstrates large model-size/energy reduction at fixed accuracy versus homogeneous quantization (see paper).

## Main results
The heterogeneously quantized models retain accuracy close to the floating-point baseline while substantially reducing energy and footprint relative to uniform quantization (quantitative energy/size/accuracy comparisons are in the paper). The approach enables nanosecond-latency inference suitable for the LHC trigger.

## Relevance to BNJetTag
QKeras is the QAT engine BNJetTag uses to train binary {−1,+1} weights, so this paper is the methodological backbone of our quantization-aware training. AutoQKeras's per-layer heterogeneous bit-width search is directly relevant to deciding which parts of our transformer can go fully binary versus needing a few extra bits, and QTools's energy/size objective mirrors our resource-budget goals. The demonstration that aggressive quantization preserves accuracy at nanosecond latency underpins our claim that 1-bit weights can stay near full-precision tagging efficiency while collapsing DSP usage.
