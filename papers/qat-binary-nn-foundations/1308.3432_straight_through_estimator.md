# Estimating or Propagating Gradients Through Stochastic Neurons for Conditional Computation

- **Authors:** Yoshua Bengio, Nicholas Léonard, Aaron Courville
- **Venue / Year:** arXiv 2013 (technical report; not formally published at a venue)
- **arXiv:** 1308.3432 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.3432
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1308.3432
- **Code:** none found (original; conceptual paper)
- **Topic area:** QAT & binary-NN foundations

## TL;DR
This is the paper that names and analyzes the **straight-through estimator (STE)**: when a neuron is stochastic or non-differentiable (e.g. a threshold/sign), back-propagate by treating the hard nonlinearity as the identity, passing the incoming gradient straight through. It compares STE against unbiased but high-variance estimators (REINFORCE) and shows STE is a practical, low-variance (biased) choice for training networks with discrete/binary units.

## Summary
The paper studies how to estimate or propagate gradients through stochastic or hard-threshold ("non-smooth") neurons, the obstacle to training networks with discrete decisions such as conditional computation and binary units. It compares four families of estimators, including the minimum-variance unbiased estimator for stochastic binary neurons (a special case of REINFORCE) and a decomposition of a binary stochastic neuron into a stochastic binary part plus a smooth differentiable part. Crucially it introduces the straight-through estimator — back-propagating through the hard threshold as though it were the identity — and finds it works well in practice despite being biased.

## Key methods / contributions
- Introduces and names the **straight-through estimator (STE)** for hard-threshold / stochastic binary units.
- Surveys four estimator families: REINFORCE-style unbiased estimators, decomposition into stochastic + smooth parts, injecting noise before the nonlinearity, and the straight-through estimator.
- Analyzes the bias/variance trade-off: STE is biased but low-variance and cheap; REINFORCE is unbiased but high-variance.
- Motivated by conditional computation (sparsely activating parts of a network), foreshadowing later sparsity/MoE work.

## Main results
Empirically, the straight-through and related estimators allow training of networks with stochastic/binary neurons and tend to outperform the high-variance unbiased estimators in the regimes studied (qualitative; see paper for specifics).

## Relevance to BNJetTag
This is the theoretical license for everything QAT in BNJetTag. Binarizing weights/activations uses a sign/threshold whose true derivative is zero almost everywhere; the **straight-through estimator** introduced here is exactly how QKeras (and BitNet) push gradients through that sign during quantization-aware training. Whenever our reports say "we rely on the straight-through estimator," this 2013 paper is the primary citation. It underpins both our binary {−1,+1} weights and the multi-bit activation quantizers in the A8/A6/A4 sweep, all of which need a surrogate gradient through a non-differentiable rounding/sign step.
