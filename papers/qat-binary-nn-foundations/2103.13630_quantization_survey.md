# A Survey of Quantization Methods for Efficient Neural Network Inference

- **Authors:** Amir Gholami, Sehoon Kim, Zhen Dong, Zhewei Yao, Michael W. Mahoney, Kurt Keutzer (UC Berkeley)
- **Venue / Year:** arXiv 2021 (later a chapter in *Low-Power Computer Vision*, Chapman & Hall/CRC, 2022)
- **arXiv:** 2103.13630 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.13630
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.13630
- **Code:** none found (survey)
- **Topic area:** QAT & binary-NN foundations

## TL;DR
A comprehensive, well-organized survey of neural-network quantization for efficient inference. It frames quantization as distributing continuous values over a discrete set, and systematically covers uniform vs. non-uniform, symmetric vs. asymmetric, static vs. dynamic, per-tensor vs. per-channel schemes, plus **post-training quantization (PTQ)** vs. **quantization-aware training (QAT)** and extreme/binary quantization.

## Summary
The survey reviews methods for moving neural networks from floating-point to low-precision (down to ≤4 bits and binary) representations to reduce memory, bandwidth, and compute. It organizes the field around core design axes — quantization granularity, uniform vs. non-uniform grids, symmetric/asymmetric mappings, calibration, and the choice between post-training quantization and quantization-aware training — and discusses the straight-through estimator, mixed-precision and hardware-aware quantization, and simulated vs. integer-only inference. It is widely used as a reference map of the quantization literature, situating methods like PACT, LSQ, and binary networks within a common framework.

## Key methods / contributions
- A taxonomy of quantization: uniform/non-uniform, symmetric/asymmetric, static/dynamic, per-tensor/per-channel.
- Clear treatment of **PTQ vs. QAT**, including when each is appropriate and their accuracy/cost trade-offs.
- Discussion of the straight-through estimator and surrogate gradients for training quantized nets.
- Coverage of mixed-precision, hardware-aware, and extreme (binary/ternary) quantization.
- Practical guidance on simulated quantization vs. integer-only inference and deployment considerations.

## Main results
A survey: it consolidates and compares published results rather than producing new benchmarks. Use it as a structured reference; cite the primary papers for specific numbers.

## Relevance to BNJetTag
This is our go-to map for placing BNJetTag's choices in the broader quantization landscape and for getting terminology right in REPORT.md (e.g., per-tensor vs. per-channel, symmetric vs. asymmetric, PTQ vs. QAT). It explains why we do **QAT** (not PTQ) for an extreme {−1,+1} weight network — at binary precision, post-training quantization collapses accuracy and training-time simulation of the quantizer is essential. Its sections on the straight-through estimator and on extreme/binary quantization directly justify our methodology, and its uniform-quantizer framing helps describe the A8/A6/A4 activation sweep precisely.
