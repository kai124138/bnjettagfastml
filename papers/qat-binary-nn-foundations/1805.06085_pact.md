# PACT: Parameterized Clipping Activation for Quantized Neural Networks

- **Authors:** Jungwook Choi, Zhuo Wang, Swagath Venkataramani, Pierce I-Jen Chuang, Vijayalakshmi Srinivasan, Kailash Gopalakrishnan
- **Venue / Year:** arXiv 2018 (IBM Research; widely cited preprint)
- **arXiv:** 1805.06085 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.06085
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.06085
- **Code:** none found (official); reimplemented in several QAT libraries
- **Topic area:** QAT & binary-NN foundations

## TL;DR
PACT makes the activation clipping threshold a **learnable parameter** α, trained by gradient descent jointly with the weights. By learning where to clip before uniform quantization, it sets the right dynamic range per layer and enables 4-bit weights *and* activations with accuracy comparable to full precision — solving the activation-quantization side of the problem that weight-only methods left open.

## Summary
The paper observes that most quantization work focused on weights, while activations (which have an unbounded range from ReLU) are harder to quantize at low precision. PACT (PArameterized Clipping acTivation) replaces ReLU with a clipping activation whose upper bound α is learned during training, then uniformly quantizes the clipped output. Learning α balances clipping error against quantization error per layer. The authors show that with PACT, both weights and activations can be quantized to 4 bits while matching full-precision accuracy across a range of models and datasets — the first such demonstration at that precision at the time.

## Key methods / contributions
- Replaces ReLU with a parameterized clipping activation bounded by a learnable scalar α per layer.
- Learns α by backpropagation (gradient is zero where the activation is clipped, straight-through through the quantizer), trading off clipping vs. quantization error.
- Adds an L2 regularizer on α to encourage tighter ranges.
- Enables uniform low-bit activation quantization combined with low-bit weights.
- Demonstrates 4-bit weight + 4-bit activation networks near full-precision accuracy.

## Main results
Reports that 4-bit weights and activations achieve accuracy comparable to full-precision baselines across several popular CNNs/datasets, outperforming prior fixed-clipping quantization schemes (exact top-1 numbers per model: see paper).

## Relevance to BNJetTag
PACT is the conceptual backbone of our **activation precision sweep (A8/A6/A4)**. While BitNet/BinaryConnect handle the {−1,+1} weight side, activations still need a sensible range and bit-width; PACT's learnable clipping is exactly the kind of mechanism (mirrored by QKeras `quantized_relu`/clip parameters) that lets us drop activation precision toward 4 bits without wrecking tagging efficiency. Its core finding — that 4-bit activations can match full precision when the clip range is learned — directly supports our hypothesis that an A4 (or A6) BNJetTag tagger can stay near full-precision AUC while shrinking FPGA resource usage.
