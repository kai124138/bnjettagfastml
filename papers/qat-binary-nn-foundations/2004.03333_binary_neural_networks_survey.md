# Binary Neural Networks: A Survey

- **Authors:** Haotong Qin, Ruihao Gong, Xianglong Liu, Xiao Bai, Jingkuan Song, Nicu Sebe
- **Venue / Year:** Pattern Recognition, vol. 105, art. 107281, 2020
- **arXiv:** 2004.03333 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03333
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.03333
- **Code:** none found (survey)
- **Topic area:** QAT & binary-NN foundations

## TL;DR
A dedicated survey of **binary neural networks** (1-bit weights/activations). It organizes BNN algorithms into "native" methods that binarize directly and "optimized" methods that reduce quantization error, improve the loss, or reduce gradient error, and it covers hardware-friendly design and practical training tricks — making it the single best entry point to the binary-NN literature that BitNet descends from.

## Summary
The survey explains that binarizing weights and activations to a single bit yields large storage and compute savings, making BNNs attractive for resource-limited devices, but that binarization causes severe information loss and its discontinuity makes optimization hard. It presents a comprehensive review of BNN algorithms grouped into native solutions (direct binarization) and optimized solutions that minimize quantization error, redesign the loss function, or reduce gradient/estimator error. It further discusses hardware-friendly designs (e.g. XNOR/popcount), training tricks, evaluation on common benchmarks, and open challenges and future directions for the field.

## Key methods / contributions
- Taxonomy of BNN methods: **native** (direct sign-based binarization) vs. **optimized** (error/loss/gradient-aware).
- Reviews quantization-error minimization (scaling factors à la XNOR-Net), loss-function improvements, and gradient/STE refinements.
- Surveys hardware-friendly aspects: bitwise XNOR + popcount kernels and deployment on constrained devices.
- Catalogs training tricks (e.g. real-valued latent weights, careful BN/activation ordering, two-stage training).
- Benchmarks the landscape and outlines open problems and future directions.

## Main results
A survey: it compiles and compares accuracy/efficiency results across the BNN literature rather than reporting new experiments (consult cited works for specific numbers).

## Relevance to BNJetTag
This is the most focused reference for our exact regime — 1-bit, {−1,+1} networks — and the natural literature anchor for BitNet's binary weights in BNJetTag. Its native-vs-optimized framing helps us position our tagger: we use plain sign binarization for weights (native) but lean on optimization-side tricks (latent full-precision weights, the straight-through estimator, multi-bit activations via the A8/A6/A4 sweep) to recover accuracy. The survey's hardware-friendly-design section (XNOR/popcount → bitwise logic) is directly aligned with our core claim that binary weights map to FPGA LUT/logic instead of DSP multipliers in the CMS L1 trigger.
