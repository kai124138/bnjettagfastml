# Ternary Weight Networks

- **Authors:** Fengfu Li, Bin Liu, Xiaoxing Wang, Bo Zhang, Junchi Yan (v2 author list; the original v1 was Fengfu Li, Bo Zhang)
- **Venue / Year:** arXiv 2016 (v1); revised v2 later. Also appeared as a workshop/ICASSP-line contribution. Chinese Academy of Sciences.
- **arXiv:** 1605.04711 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.04711
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.04711
- **Code:** Author Caffe implementation (TWN): https://github.com/fengfu-chris/caffe-twns
- **Topic area:** BitNet & 1-bit/ternary quantization

## TL;DR
A foundational pre-LLM paper that constrains network weights to **ternary {−1, 0, +1}** with a single scaling factor. It minimizes the Euclidean distance between full-precision and ternary weights and uses a **threshold-based ternary function** (weights below a threshold Δ go to 0). Ternary is shown to be more expressive than binary while still being ~16× smaller than FP32 and nearly multiply-free.

## Summary
Ternary Weight Networks (TWNs) constrain weights to {−1, 0, +1}, sitting between binary-weight networks and full precision. Training minimizes the Euclidean (L2) distance between the full-precision weights and the scaled ternary weights; a threshold-based ternary function provides a fast, approximate closed-form solution (a symmetric threshold Δ, roughly 0.7·mean(|W|), decides which weights become 0 vs ±1, and a per-layer scale α restores magnitude). TWNs achieve up to 16× model compression and replace most multiplications with additions, while showing better accuracy than binary-weight networks. Experiments on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet show TWN accuracy close to full precision and clearly ahead of binary-weight counterparts.

## Key methods / contributions
- **Ternary {−1, 0, +1} weight constraint** with a **per-layer scaling factor α** to preserve magnitude.
- **Threshold-based ternarization:** symmetric threshold Δ (≈ 0.7·E[|W|]) maps small weights to 0, the rest to ±α.
- Formulated as **minimizing L2 distance** between full-precision and ternary weights → fast approximate solution.
- **Higher expressive capacity than binary** (the extra "0" state) at minimal extra cost; ~16× compression vs FP32.
- Near **multiply-free inference** (mostly additions), a key efficiency property.

## Main results
On MNIST, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet, TWNs outperform Binary-Weight-Networks and approach full-precision accuracy (very close on MNIST and CIFAR-10), at up to 16× compression with far fewer multiplications (see paper for exact accuracy/error figures). The qualitative conclusion: the ternary "0" state buys meaningful accuracy over pure binary at negligible cost.

## Relevance to BNJetTag
This is one of the two classical roots of the ternary idea that BitNet b1.58 later scaled to LLMs, and it is directly informative for the **binary-vs-ternary** design question in BNJetTag. The threshold + per-layer scaling-factor recipe is essentially what modern BitLinear/absmean quantization formalizes, so it grounds our QAT weight-quantization design. For our FPGA mapping: TWN's "mostly additions, almost no multiplies" is the same DSP-free argument we make — ternary weights compile to add/subtract/skip rather than DSP multipliers. If BNJetTag ever trades pure binary {−1,+1} for ternary to recover tagging AUC, this paper is the canonical justification and a source for the thresholding/scaling mechanics (and the FPGA cost of representing/skipping the "0" state).
