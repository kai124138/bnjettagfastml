# BitNet a4.8: 4-bit Activations for 1-bit LLMs

- **Authors:** Hongyu Wang, Shuming Ma, Furu Wei
- **Venue / Year:** arXiv 2024 (Microsoft Research / UCAS)
- **arXiv:** 2411.04965 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04965
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.04965
- **Code:** No official Microsoft repo found at brief time; community reimplementation: https://github.com/Agora-Lab-AI/BitNet-a4.8 . Related official: https://github.com/microsoft/BitNet
- **Topic area:** BitNet & 1-bit/ternary quantization

## TL;DR
BitNet a4.8 pushes activation precision of 1-bit (b1.58 ternary-weight) LLMs down to **4 bits**. It uses a **hybrid quantization + sparsification** strategy to absorb the outlier-channel errors that normally break low-bit activations: 4-bit (INT4/FP4) activations into the attention and FFN inputs, but **sparsify-then-8-bit** for sensitive intermediate states. Performance stays comparable to b1.58 at the same training cost, with faster INT4/FP4 inference.

## Summary
Continuing the 1-bit LLM line (BitNet b1.58), this paper enables 4-bit activations. The central problem is that a few outlier activation channels carry large magnitudes and dominate quantization error at low bit-widths. BitNet a4.8 mitigates this with a hybrid scheme: it applies 4-bit activation quantization to the inputs of attention and feed-forward layers, while the more sensitive intermediate states are sparsified and then quantized to 8 bits. The result matches BitNet b1.58 accuracy at equivalent training cost while being faster at inference via 4-bit kernels. It additionally activates only ~55% of parameters and supports a 3-bit KV cache, further improving large-scale deployment efficiency.

## Key methods / contributions
- **4-bit activations (INT4/FP4)** for the inputs to attention and FFN layers in a 1.58-bit (ternary-weight) LLM.
- **Hybrid quantization + sparsification:** outlier-heavy intermediate states are **sparsified then 8-bit quantized** instead of forced to 4-bit, controlling outlier error.
- **Two-stage / mixed-precision activation recipe** so different tensors get different bit-widths based on sensitivity.
- **~55% parameter activation** (effective sparsity) and **3-bit KV cache** support for cheaper inference.
- Enables practical **INT4/FP4 kernels** → faster decoding than b1.58 at comparable accuracy and training cost.

## Main results
BitNet a4.8 is reported to achieve performance comparable to BitNet b1.58 at equivalent training cost, while delivering faster inference through 4-bit activation kernels, ~55% active parameters, and 3-bit KV cache (see paper for exact perplexity/zero-shot accuracy and speed numbers). The takeaway: 4-bit activations are feasible for 1-bit-weight LLMs *if* outlier channels are handled with hybrid precision/sparsity rather than uniform 4-bit.

## Relevance to BNJetTag
This is the **most directly relevant paper to BNJetTag's activation-quantization axis (A8→A6→A4).** It is concrete evidence that you can drop 1-bit/ternary-weight transformer activations to 4 bits without losing accuracy — but only with care around outlier channels. For our FPGA tagger, A4 activations mean narrower datapaths and smaller LUT/accumulator usage, directly improving the resource/latency budget for the CMS L1 trigger. The hybrid "most tensors at 4-bit, sensitive ones at 8-bit + sparsify" recipe is a template for our own A4 study: if uniform A4 hurts jet-tagging AUC, BNJetTag could selectively keep specific layers/states at higher precision. Note their outlier story is for billion-param LLMs; our tagger is tiny, so the outlier severity (and whether we even need the sparsification step) must be re-measured on jet data.
