# The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits

- **Authors:** Shuming Ma, Hongyu Wang, Lingxiao Ma, Lei Wang, Wenhui Wang, Shaohan Huang, Li Dong, Ruiping Wang, Jilong Xue, Furu Wei
- **Venue / Year:** arXiv 2024 (Microsoft Research / UCAS)
- **arXiv:** 2402.17764 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17764
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17764
- **Code:** Official inference framework (later): https://github.com/microsoft/BitNet (bitnet.cpp). FAQ/training tips: https://github.com/microsoft/unilm/tree/master/bitnet
- **Topic area:** BitNet & 1-bit/ternary quantization

## TL;DR
Introduces **BitNet b1.58**, where every weight is **ternary {−1, 0, +1}** (~1.58 bits). It matches full-precision (FP16/BF16) Transformers of the same size and training-token budget in perplexity and end-task accuracy, while being far cheaper in latency, memory, throughput, and energy. The ternary scheme adds a "0" state (feature selection) on top of pure binary, and the multiply-free matmul is the headline efficiency win.

## Summary
Building on BitNet, this paper proposes BitNet b1.58, a 1.58-bit LLM variant in which all parameters are ternary {−1, 0, +1}. Trained from scratch, b1.58 matches the full-precision Transformer baseline (FP16/BF16) at equal model size and training tokens on both perplexity and downstream task performance, while delivering large gains in latency, memory, throughput, and energy. The authors argue that ternary weights turn matrix multiplication into essentially integer addition (no floating-point multiplies), defining a new scaling law and training recipe and motivating dedicated hardware for 1-bit/1.58-bit models.

## Key methods / contributions
- **Ternary weights {−1, 0, +1}** (≈1.58 bits) via an **absmean** weight-quantization function; the extra "0" enables implicit feature filtering vs pure binary.
- **Multiply-free linear layers:** matmul reduces to additions/subtractions, removing FP multipliers — directly relevant to hardware.
- **Matches FP16/BF16** baselines at the *same* parameter count and training tokens (perplexity + end tasks), not just "close."
- Demonstrates a **new scaling law/recipe** for high-performance, cost-effective LLMs.
- Argues for **new computation paradigms / specialized hardware** optimized for 1-bit/1.58-bit weights.
- Pairs ternary weights with **8-bit activations** (absmax per-token quantization), continuing the BitNet QAT-from-scratch approach.

## Main results
BitNet b1.58 is reported to match full-precision LLaMA-style baselines in perplexity and downstream accuracy starting around the 3B parameter scale, with growing advantages in memory, latency, throughput, and energy as size increases (see paper for exact perplexity, speedup, and memory-reduction figures at 700M/1.3B/3B/3.9B). Crossover to matching/beating FP16 occurs at larger sizes.

## Relevance to BNJetTag
This is the closest precedent for any **ternary** variant of BNJetTag. Where our default tagger uses binary {−1,+1}, b1.58 shows that allowing a "0" state (ternary) can recover full-precision accuracy and may improve the AUC-vs-resource trade-off — at the cost that "0" weights still need representation/skip logic on FPGA. Its core claim — ternary/binary weights make matmul *multiply-free* (adds only) — is precisely why BNJetTag expects the binary core to consume ~0 DSP and map to LUT/logic instead. The paper's reliance on **A8** activations is our baseline; our A6/A4 sweep extends past it. Useful as the reference point for arguing binary vs ternary in the thesis.
