# BitNet: Scaling 1-bit Transformers for Large Language Models

- **Authors:** Hongyu Wang, Shuming Ma, Li Dong, Shaohan Huang, Huaijie Wang, Lingxiao Ma, Fan Yang, Ruiping Wang, Yi Wu, Furu Wei
- **Venue / Year:** arXiv 2023 (Microsoft Research / UCAS / Tsinghua)
- **arXiv:** 2310.11453 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11453
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11453
- **Code:** No official repo at release; community reimplementations exist (e.g. https://github.com/kyegomez/BitNet). Microsoft's official BitNet inference framework (bitnet.cpp) came later: https://github.com/microsoft/BitNet
- **Topic area:** BitNet & 1-bit/ternary quantization

## TL;DR
BitNet is the foundational 1-bit Transformer architecture for LLMs. Its core component, **BitLinear**, is a drop-in replacement for `nn.Linear` that trains binary {−1,+1} weights *from scratch* (quantization-aware training), rather than post-training quantizing a full-precision model. It matches the trend of FP16 baselines while drastically cutting memory and energy versus 8-bit methods.

## Summary
The paper tackles the deployment and energy cost of ever-larger LLMs by introducing a scalable, stable 1-bit Transformer. The key building block, BitLinear, replaces standard linear layers so 1-bit weights can be learned during training. Activations are quantized (the original BitNet uses 8-bit activations), and a straight-through estimator handles gradient flow through the non-differentiable sign/quantization functions. Experiments on language modeling show BitNet achieves competitive perplexity and downstream performance while substantially reducing memory footprint and energy consumption relative to state-of-the-art 8-bit quantization and FP16 Transformer baselines, and it exhibits favorable scaling behavior as model size grows.

## Key methods / contributions
- **BitLinear layer:** drop-in replacement for `nn.Linear` with binarized {−1,+1} weights, trained from scratch (not post-training quantization).
- **Weight binarization via sign** with a learned/absmean scaling factor to preserve weight variance/energy.
- **Quantization-aware training (QAT)** using the straight-through estimator (STE) so gradients pass through the binarization.
- **8-bit activation quantization** (absmax/per-tensor) paired with the binary weights; LayerNorm placement adapted for training stability.
- **Scaling-law evidence:** BitNet follows a scaling law similar to full-precision Transformers, so benefits hold (and grow) at larger model sizes.
- Reported large reductions in memory and energy vs FP16 and 8-bit baselines.

## Main results
On language modeling, BitNet reports competitive perplexity and downstream task accuracy versus FP16 Transformers and superior efficiency/accuracy trade-offs versus 8-bit quantization baselines, with substantial memory and energy savings (see paper for exact perplexity, energy, and scaling-curve numbers). The paper emphasizes that the accuracy gap to full precision narrows as model size increases.

## Relevance to BNJetTag
This is the *direct conceptual ancestor* of BNJetTag's binary-weight transformer tagger. BitLinear is exactly the mechanism we adapt: {−1,+1} weights trained from scratch with STE-based QAT. The central BNJetTag thesis — that binary weights eliminate the need for DSP multipliers (sign-flip + add maps to FPGA LUTs/logic) — is the hardware corollary of BitNet's "1-bit weights replace FP16 multiplies" claim. BitNet's use of **8-bit activations** is our A8 baseline; our activation-quantization sweep (A8→A6→A4) probes how far below BitNet's activation precision we can push before tagging AUC, FPGA resources, or L1-trigger latency degrade.
