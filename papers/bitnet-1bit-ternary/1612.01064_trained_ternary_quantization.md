# Trained Ternary Quantization

- **Authors:** Chenzhuo Zhu, Song Han, Huizi Mao, William J. Dally
- **Venue / Year:** ICLR 2017 (conference paper / poster); arXiv 2016. Stanford / Tsinghua / NVIDIA.
- **arXiv:** 1612.01064 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01064
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.01064
- **Code:** Official implementation (tensorpack-based): https://github.com/czhu95/ternarynet
- **Topic area:** BitNet & 1-bit/ternary quantization

## TL;DR
Trained Ternary Quantization (TTQ) reduces weights to **ternary {−Wn, 0, +Wp}** but, unlike fixed-scale ternary methods, it **learns two independent, asymmetric scaling coefficients (Wn, Wp) per layer by gradient descent**. This learned-scale approach gives almost no accuracy loss — and on several CIFAR-10/ImageNet models it *beats* the full-precision baseline — while keeping ~16× compression and 2-bit weight storage.

## Summary
TTQ targets deployment of large models under tight power/memory budgets on mobile devices. It quantizes weights to ternary values but makes the two nonzero scale factors learnable and asymmetric (a positive scale Wp and a negative scale Wn per layer), trained jointly with the network via backprop and a straight-through-style gradient. The method learns both the ternary *values* (the scales) and the ternary *assignment* (which weights are −/0/+). At inference only ternary values (2-bit weights) plus the per-layer scales are needed, giving ~16× size reduction. Reported results show negligible accuracy degradation and, for some models (ResNet-32/44/56 on CIFAR-10, AlexNet on ImageNet), accuracy exceeding the full-precision baseline and prior ternary methods.

## Key methods / contributions
- **Learned, asymmetric per-layer scales (Wp, Wn)** for the +1 and −1 ternary states, optimized by gradient descent (vs fixed/symmetric scales in TWN).
- **Jointly learns ternary values and ternary assignment** during training (normalize → threshold → scale).
- **Straight-through gradient** routing to the latent full-precision weights and to the scaling coefficients.
- **2-bit weights + per-layer scales at inference**, ~16× compression, multiply-light.
- Shows ternary nets can **match or exceed full precision**, not merely approximate it.

## Main results
TTQ reports very little accuracy degradation and, in several cases, improvements over the full-precision model: per the paper, it outperforms prior state-of-the-art ternary networks on AlexNet (ImageNet) by ~3.0% Top-1 and exceeds the full-precision AlexNet by ~1.6%, with gains also on ResNet-32/44/56 (CIFAR-10) (see paper for the full table). Qualitative takeaway: learning the ternary scales, rather than fixing them, closes — and can reverse — the accuracy gap.

## Relevance to BNJetTag
TTQ is the second classical ternary root and the clearest argument that **how you choose the quantization scale matters as much as the bit-width** — a lesson that carries straight into BNJetTag's BitLinear/absmean design and our QAT/STE pipeline. The idea of *learnable* scaling factors (here asymmetric Wp/Wn) is a knob we can consider if a fixed absmean scale leaves tagging AUC on the table. For the FPGA story, ternary weights with a per-layer scale are still essentially multiply-free in the core (sign-select + accumulate, scale applied once per layer), reinforcing the DSP-free thesis. Authorship by Han/Dally also ties this work to the broader efficient-inference / hardware-codesign lineage that BNJetTag sits in. As with TWN, the practical caveat for us is the FPGA cost of the "0" state vs pure binary {−1,+1}.
