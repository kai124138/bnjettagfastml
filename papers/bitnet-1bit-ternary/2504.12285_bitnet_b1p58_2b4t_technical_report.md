# BitNet b1.58 2B4T Technical Report

- **Authors:** Shuming Ma, Hongyu Wang, Shaohan Huang, Xingxing Zhang, Ying Hu, Ting Song, Yan Xia, Furu Wei
- **Venue / Year:** arXiv 2025 (Microsoft Research)
- **arXiv:** 2504.12285 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12285
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.12285
- **Code / Weights:** Model weights on Hugging Face: https://huggingface.co/microsoft/bitnet-b1.58-2B-4T ; official inference (GPU + CPU): https://github.com/microsoft/BitNet
- **Topic area:** BitNet & 1-bit/ternary quantization

## TL;DR
The first **open-source, native 1-bit LLM at the 2B-parameter scale**, trained from scratch on **4 trillion tokens** ("2B4T"). It reaches performance on par with leading open-weight *full-precision* models of similar size, while slashing memory, energy, and decoding latency. This is the proof-at-scale that native 1-bit (ternary b1.58) training — not post-training quantization — works in a real, released model.

## Summary
This technical report presents BitNet b1.58 2B4T, a 2-billion-parameter model with native 1.58-bit (ternary {−1,0,+1}) weights trained from scratch on a 4-trillion-token corpus. It is evaluated across language understanding, mathematical reasoning, coding, and conversational benchmarks, and is reported to match leading open-weight full-precision LLMs of comparable size while offering large efficiency advantages: reduced memory footprint, lower energy consumption, and lower decoding latency. The authors release open weights and open-source inference implementations for both GPU and CPU, making it the first openly available native 1-bit LLM at this scale. Emphasis is on training natively in 1-bit from the start to avoid the accuracy loss seen when quantizing a full-precision model after training.

## Key methods / contributions
- **Native 1.58-bit (ternary) training from scratch** at 2B params on a 4T-token corpus — not post-training quantization.
- **First open-source native 1-bit LLM at the 2B scale**, with released weights and inference code.
- Cross-domain evaluation: language understanding, math reasoning, coding, conversation.
- **Open GPU + CPU inference implementations** (ties into bitnet.cpp), showing 1-bit LLMs run efficiently even on commodity CPUs.
- Demonstrates competitiveness vs full-precision open-weight peers and vs INT4 post-training-quantized models of similar size.

## Main results
BitNet b1.58 2B4T is reported to achieve performance comparable to leading open-weight full-precision LLMs of similar size across the benchmark suite, while maintaining stronger overall performance than INT4-quantized counterparts (e.g. of a comparable ~1.5B model). Search results cite top-2 average-benchmark ranking among compared models and category leads such as GSM8K (58.38) and WinoGrande (71.90); treat exact per-task numbers as from the report (see paper). Efficiency: substantially reduced memory, energy, and decoding latency vs full-precision baselines.

## Relevance to BNJetTag
This report is the strongest existence proof that **native 1-bit training scales and stays accurate** — the load-bearing assumption behind BNJetTag's "binary core with ~0 DSP, near-full-precision AUC" thesis. Two takeaways for us: (1) train natively in the low-bit regime from scratch (as we do) rather than quantizing a trained FP32 tagger — the report explicitly motivates this to avoid accuracy loss, validating our QAT/STE pipeline; (2) the CPU-efficiency result echoes our FPGA argument: when weights are ternary/binary, the multiply-free arithmetic maps to cheap logic. While this is a billion-scale NLP model (vs our tiny jet-tagging transformer), it anchors the claim that the BitNet family does not trade away accuracy for its hardware savings — useful framing for REPORT.md and the presentation when justifying the binary-weight design choice.
